# AI_investing V3.8.1 Phase 4 Threshold Analysis Report

Analysis date: 2026-08-12

## Scope and method

This is sensitivity evidence, not threshold optimization.

- Universe: current 19-symbol configured watchlist.
- Data: canonical local OHLCV, unchanged.
- Eligible history: 4,604 date-symbol observations, 249 trading dates from
  2025-08-14 through 2026-08-11.
- Score: current production implementation reconstructed cross-sectionally for
  each date, including date-local RS percentile.
- A “signal” in this table means `FinalScore >= threshold` only. It does not
  apply the production BUY filters and is not an entry-transition count.
- Missing forward returns near the end of the sample are excluded from the
  relevant average/win-rate metric and are never filled.
- Win rate is the percentage of valid 20-day forward returns above zero.
- Volatility is sample standard deviation of individual 20-day forward returns.
- Maximum drawdown is a diagnostic proxy: date-level mean 20-day cohort returns
  are converted to daily-equivalent returns and compounded by signal date. It
  is not the drawdown of an executable portfolio because cohorts overlap and no
  sizing/capital constraint is modeled.

The reproducible output is `results/score_threshold_analysis.csv`.

## Observations, not optimization conclusions

- Counts fall rapidly: threshold 75 retains only 2.37% of eligible
  observations; 80 retains 0.89%; 85 and 90 have only 10 and 1 observations.
- Average forward return and win rate generally improve from 50 through 70,
  but the relationship is not monotonic beyond 70.
- Threshold 70 has the highest observed average 5D/20D/60D returns among rows
  with a material sample, but it also has the highest 20D volatility and the
  worst drawdown proxy in the table. This is not evidence to change the
  production threshold.
- The 75 threshold has a smaller sample than 70, similar 20-day average return,
  a modestly higher win rate, and lower measured volatility in this one-year
  current-universe sample.
- Results at 85 and 90 are statistically fragile and should not guide policy.
- The analysis uses the current watchlist historically, creating survivorship
  and selection bias. Signals overlap, transaction costs are absent, and market
  regimes are limited. These constraints prevent a defensible “best threshold”
  conclusion.

## Known Phase 3 findings

### RiskScore

RiskScore equals 50 for all 4,604 observations. It adds:

- 7.5 points to RawScore (`50 × 0.15`), and
- 5.25 points to FinalScore (`7.5 × 0.70`).

It cannot change cross-sectional rank on a date, but it lowers the amount that
all other components must contribute to cross a fixed signal threshold. At BUY
threshold 75, the non-RiskScore contribution only needs to reach 69.75.
For scale only—not as a removal proposal—220 observations score at least 69.75,
versus 109 at least 75. Removing or changing RiskScore would therefore require
explicit threshold recalibration. Phase 4 leaves it untouched.

### VolumeScore

Historical distribution:

- 0 points: 2,792 observations (60.64%)
- 10 points: 1,446 (31.41%)
- 20 points: 366 (7.95%)

Historical Volume_Ratio has mean 1.003, median 0.925, P5 0.564, P95 1.703, and
range 0.106–5.476. Therefore, the all-zero current 19-symbol snapshot is not an
absence of historical volume variation: it is principally a current-date
condition in which all latest ratios are at or below 1.0. The formula is also
coarse—only three discrete states—so it discards within-band variation. No
formula change is made.

### Momentum

Across the historical eligible observations:

- Mean absolute MACD contribution: 20.88 points.
- Mean absolute return contribution: 13.05 points.
- MACD is larger in absolute value for 2,672/4,604 observations (58.04%);
  return contribution is larger for 1,932 (41.96%).
- MomentumScore correlation is 0.798 with MACD contribution and 0.852 with
  return contribution.

MACD has concentration risk because three binary tests collapse into four
discrete contribution levels and can contribute up to 40 points. Historical
evidence nevertheless shows that continuous returns remain a major source of
differentiation and are more correlated with total MomentumScore in this
sample. The evidence supports continued review, not a weight change.

## Recommendation

**ADDITIONAL ALIGNMENT WORK REQUIRED BEFORE THRESHOLD CALIBRATION.** Keep the
current thresholds unchanged. First establish a historically faithful,
versioned production-signal backtest using the same FinalScore and filters;
then evaluate thresholds across broader, point-in-time universes and multiple
market regimes with non-overlapping or portfolio-realistic returns, costs, and
out-of-sample validation.

The conservative conclusion is to avoid optimizing against a small,
survivorship-biased sample. The largest risk here is false precision, not a
failure to choose a new number.

# AI_investing V3.8.1 Phase 6B-8D-1 Historical Evidence Collection Plan

Plan date: 2026-08-13

Status: design only. This plan introduces no production code, pipeline change,
synthetic production evidence, portfolio action, or trading authority.

# 1. Purpose

Phase 6B-8D-1 defines how AI_investing will collect real historical shadow
evidence before any portfolio-pipeline cutover can be considered.

Historical evidence is required because a safe implementation is not the same
as an observed production history. Software validation can show that code
rejects malformed data, preserves schemas, joins contracts correctly, and
fails closed under controlled inputs. Production evidence must additionally
show what the governed candidate, risk, portfolio, and validation path actually
produced at real decision dates using only information available at those
dates.

Synthetic fixtures and unit tests remain necessary testing evidence, but they
cannot prove:

- that real upstream artifacts were available and internally consistent;
- that real market and portfolio data respected the decision-time boundary;
- that operational runs produced stable provenance and complete risk coverage;
- that adverse and no-action states were preserved rather than repaired; or
- that the new path behaves consistently across actual dates and regimes.

Therefore, a test PASS does not satisfy a historical-evidence requirement and
cannot approve a portfolio cutover. This phase plans evidence collection only;
it does not approve migration.

# 2. Evidence Principles

## 2.1 Immutable evidence

Every archive record is an exact, append-only record of one completed
historical shadow run. Captured candidate, risk, portfolio, and validation
artifacts must not be recalculated, normalized, edited, merged, or repaired by
the collection process.

## 2.2 Point-in-time correctness

AsOfDate is the economic decision date. All market observations, indicators,
risk observations, and portfolio state used by the archived run must have been
available by that date. Future OHLCV, future holdings, completed-trade metrics,
publication data not yet available, nearest-date substitution, and forward-fill
repair are forbidden.

## 2.3 Reproducibility

Identical governed inputs and versions must produce identical candidate
ordering, eligibility, risk classification, validation outcome, and content
hashes. Informational capture timestamps may differ but must not affect RunId,
ranking, risk status, or readiness classification.

## 2.4 Provenance tracking

Each record must identify at minimum:

- RunId and AsOfDate;
- ScoreModelVersion and RiskModelVersion;
- PortfolioSnapshotId;
- repository commit and project version;
- source artifact identities;
- publisher, validator, and archive-contract versions; and
- validation result and failure reasons.

Provenance conflicts fail closed. Missing provenance must not be inferred from
filesystem timestamps or a later run.

## 2.5 SHA-256 integrity

The manifest records SHA-256 for every evidence artifact after capture. Hashes
must be calculated from the bytes that are published, and the validator must
recompute and compare them. A hash mismatch invalidates the record for
readiness evidence.

## 2.6 No overwrite

An existing canonical RunId/AsOfDate archive must never be overwritten,
updated, merged, or repaired. A failed publication leaves no canonical partial
archive. If later review finds an error, the original record remains immutable
and a separate append-only disposition records that it is excluded.

# 3. Evidence Sources

The collection process accepts only already-generated artifacts from an actual
historical shadow run.

| Evidence role | Required source | Collection rule |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate evidence | `validated_portfolio_candidates.csv` | Preserve exact candidate rows, ordering, eligibility, RunId, AsOfDate, and ScoreModelVersion |
| Risk evidence | Portfolio risk calculation output | Preserve exact point-in-time risk rows, statuses, reasons, RiskModelVersion, and portfolio snapshot provenance |
| Portfolio evidence | Portfolio snapshot output | Preserve the exact pre-action portfolio state used for risk assessment |
| Validation evidence | Validator report | Preserve contract outcomes, scenario, counts, missing-risk results, mismatches, and failure reasons |
| Manifest | Archive metadata generated during publication | Bind identity, versions, provenance, hashes, and validator result to the other artifacts |

Candidate evidence must not be sourced from legacy backtest qualification
files. Risk evidence must not recreate BacktestScore, completed-trade
MaxDrawdown, forward returns, or a fabricated RiskLevel. Portfolio evidence
must represent the snapshot used at the decision boundary, not a later
portfolio state.

# 4. Archive Structure

Historical evidence is organized under a dedicated research-only root:

```text
archive/
└── historical_shadow/
    └── YYYY-MM-DD_RUN_ID/
        ├── candidate_snapshot.csv
        ├── risk_snapshot.csv
        ├── portfolio_snapshot.csv
        ├── manifest.json
        └── validation_report.json
```

`YYYY-MM-DD` equals AsOfDate and `RUN_ID` equals the deterministic RunId in all
artifacts. Both parts must be sanitized, unambiguous, and verified against the
manifest. The directory contains exactly one candidate snapshot, one risk
snapshot, one portfolio snapshot, one manifest, and one validation report.

This collection layout is a Phase 6B-8D-1 planning contract. Before automation,
an approved implementation must define an explicit lossless mapping from the
current publisher contract (`candidates.csv`, `risk.csv`,
`portfolio_snapshot.json`, and `validation_report.md`) to these collection
names and formats. Renaming or format conversion must never alter semantic
values or make two artifact contracts appear interchangeable without a schema
version. Until that mapping is approved and validated, collection remains a
manual governance procedure and no archive is cutover evidence.

# 5. Required Historical Scenarios

Readiness review requires real, immutable evidence for every scenario below.
Absence is reported as `NOT AVAILABLE`; synthetic evidence must not fill the
gap.

| Scenario | Required real evidence | Expected safety observation |
|---|---|---|
| Zero eligible candidates | A real run with `eligible_candidates = 0` | Stable schema, no risk fabrication, and no portfolio action |
| Single eligible candidate | A real run with exactly one eligible candidate | Exact identity, deterministic rank, complete risk join, and provenance match |
| Multiple eligible candidates | A real run with more than one eligible candidate | Deterministic ordering, unique tickers, complete risk coverage, and stable rerun result |
| Risk pending/failure | Real `PENDING`, `BLOCKED`, or `FAILED` risk state | Candidate remains blocked and no portfolio action is produced |
| Metadata mismatch | A naturally occurring operational mismatch or retained failed capture | Fail-closed mismatch reason; no repair, nearest-match join, or canonical publication as PASS |
| Insufficient history | A real candidate lacking the required historical observation window | Explicit pending/blocked reason; no imputation and no fabricated risk value |
| Stale market data | A real run where observation recency violates the approved limit | Explicit stale-data failure and no promotion to READY |
| All eligible but none ready | A real run with eligible candidates but zero READY risk rows | No-action outcome with every affected ticker and reason reported |

A single record may satisfy more than one scenario only when the evidence
naturally contains those states and each state is independently reported. The
collection team must not search for favorable dates, edit states, or construct
signals to complete the matrix.

# 6. Evidence Classification

## 6.1 Production evidence

Production evidence consists of real historical shadow runs captured from the
governed candidate and point-in-time risk path. It must include immutable
artifacts, complete provenance, verified hashes, validator output, and an
auditable decision-time boundary.

Only production evidence may contribute to a future cutover-readiness evidence
matrix. Even then, evidence completeness does not itself authorize migration;
it is input to a separate governance review.

## 6.2 Testing evidence

Testing evidence includes:

- unit and integration tests;
- synthetic candidates, risks, metadata, and portfolio snapshots;
- mocks, temporary directories, mutation simulations, and concurrency tests;
- manually constructed edge cases; and
- validator and publisher fixtures.

Testing evidence establishes software behavior. It must be labeled `TESTING`
and stored outside the canonical historical evidence root. It cannot be
relabeled, copied, or counted as production evidence and cannot support
cutover approval.

# 7. Collection Workflow

```text
Historical run
    ↓
Artifact capture
    ↓
Manifest generation
    ↓
SHA-256 hashing
    ↓
Publisher
    ↓
Validator
    ↓
Readiness review
```

## 7.1 Historical run

Run the approved production-candidate, candidate-validation, point-in-time
risk, and shadow-validation path for the real AsOfDate. Freeze the exact code
versions and input boundaries. Do not replay with later data to improve an
outcome.

## 7.2 Artifact capture

Capture the exact validated candidate output, risk output, pre-action portfolio
snapshot, and validation result. Record source hashes before and after copying;
any mutation aborts the attempt.

## 7.3 Manifest generation

Create canonical metadata containing all identities, schema/model/tool
versions, repository commit, source references, row counts, scenario labels,
and failure reasons. Mixed RunId, AsOfDate, model version, or snapshot identity
fails closed.

## 7.4 SHA-256 hashing

Hash the published bytes of each artifact and record the digests in the
manifest. Re-hash immediately before publication and during every later
validation.

## 7.5 Publisher

Publish through a per-record lock and same-filesystem temporary directory.
Require atomic publication, no overwrite, source-mutation detection, and safe
cleanup. Failed attempts produce operational logs only, never partial canonical
evidence.

## 7.6 Validator

Validate required files, hashes, metadata consistency, candidate schema, risk
states, candidate-risk joins, point-in-time dates, scenario classification,
and archive immutability. A failed archive remains unavailable for positive
readiness evidence; its failure may be retained separately as operational
evidence.

## 7.7 Readiness review

Maintain a dated evidence matrix for all eight required scenarios. For each
record, cite the archive identity, validator result, provenance, observation
boundary, and reviewer disposition. Unavailable or invalid evidence remains an
explicit blocker. Reviewers must not infer success from tests or from an
unobserved scenario.

# 8. Safety Boundary

Historical shadow evidence collection is research and governance activity
only. It remains outside portfolio construction, position sizing, order draft,
order review, daily decision reporting, brokerage integration, and execution.

It must not:

- create or transmit a brokerage order;
- create, rebalance, or execute a portfolio;
- change FinalScore, TradeSignal, thresholds, eligibility, or risk formulas;
- promote a candidate or convert a validation result into an investment action;
- modify live holdings, cash, limits, or capital; or
- claim that archived performance or a validator PASS authorizes trading.

Collection has no live-capital impact. Every outcome, including READY or PASS,
means only that the captured research evidence satisfied its declared archive
contract. Portfolio cutover requires a separate, explicit human governance
decision after sufficient real multi-date evidence exists.

# 9. Completion and Governance Gate

This plan is complete when the collection contract, source boundaries, archive
layout, scenario matrix, evidence classification, workflow, and safety boundary
are approved. It does not declare the evidence collected.

Historical evidence collection may begin only after reviewers resolve the
documented format mapping between the current Phase 6B-8C publisher contract
and the Phase 6B-8D-1 archive layout. A future readiness review must classify
missing scenarios as insufficient evidence and must not approve cutover based
on software tests alone.

Decision at this phase: **PLAN DEFINED — NO CUTOVER AUTHORITY**.
